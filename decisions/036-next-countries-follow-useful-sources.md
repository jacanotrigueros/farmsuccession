# 036 — Investigate Austria, Ireland, the Netherlands and Belgium in that order

**18 October 2026.** A priority for a later expansion, not an addition to the
territory catalogue or the 2026 edition. The present scope remains the EU layer
and ES, FR, DE, IT and PT.

## The question

If readers show a need beyond the five countries, which country should receive
the next complete research pass? Row counts in a European inventory, proximity
to the current countries, and the ease of adding a language could each give a
different answer. Farm Succession's purpose is to help a person find existing
succession, land, aid and training routes, with dated evidence and a useful
original destination. It should reuse existing work where possible, rather
than build another version of a service that already serves the person.

## What the evidence said

The EU CAP Network's [assessment and Annex II](https://eu-cap-network.ec.europa.eu/publications/assessment-generational-renewal-strategies-across-eu-member-states_en)
offer a discovery inventory across 27 Member States. The `Inventory` sheet of
the Annex II workbook linked there contains 199 data rows. Filter its country
column to reproduce these counts: Austria 10, Ireland 8, Netherlands 5 and
Belgium 14 (Flanders 4, Wallonia 10). The [five-country research
memo](../research/memos/eu-generational-renewal-annex.md) explains the source,
rights and the 77 rows for the current scope. Rows include broad policies and
historical instruments: these counts are neither live services nor a coverage
score. Attribution: European Union, _Assessment of generational renewal
strategies across EU Member States_, Annex II, accessed 18 October 2026; row
selection and sequencing by Farm Succession.

The initial discovery pass also found relevant routes to check at their
original operators. It did not establish that every route is operating or that
its operator permits our intended use or links. None is admitted by this
decision.

| Order | Country         | Reason for the next research pass                                                                                        |
| ----- | --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1     | **Austria**     | Succession-specific routes deserve inspection; German is already supported, keeping the country pass smaller.            |
| 2     | **Ireland**     | Public succession-planning measures warrant inspection; English is already supported.                                    |
| 3     | **Netherlands** | Non-family succession and matching routes fit the site's purpose; adding Dutch requires a complete language pass.        |
| 4     | **Belgium**     | Regional material is substantial, but Flanders and Wallonia need separate source, territorial and language verification. |

This ordering weighs likely help to a reader and the cost of maintaining a
complete, honest country pass. It is a hypothesis, not a numerical ranking
derived from the row counts.

## The decision

If a later expansion is warranted, investigate Austria, then Ireland, then
the Netherlands, then Belgium. The order determines where to **start
verification**, not which countries automatically go live or how many resources
must be added. Do not add any of them now merely to fill a map.

For each candidate, follow every inventory lead to its original authority or
operator. Check its current service, territory, access route, reuse and linking
terms, and compare it with existing Farm Succession records and directories.
If the original does not permit the required link or use, exclude it from the
tracked repository and public site; an intermediary can remain a private
discovery aid only. A country is added with its research briefs, edition files,
catalogue entry and any complete language support together, as [023](023-a-country-is-data-a-catalogue-entry-and-a-language.md)
requires. Prefer a clear route to an existing service over duplicating its
function.

## What it costs

The first two countries are easier to present with existing languages, which
may delay a useful Dutch-language route. Belgium may contain more relevant
regional instruments than the two countries ahead of it; its 14 inventory rows
do not settle that question. A fixed sequence can become stale as services,
terms and reader demand change. A country pass also adds recurring source
checks and editorial work for one maintainer.

## What would overturn it

Revisit this order when search and usage evidence from the live site shows what
readers are seeking and why they arrive, or when verification finds a working,
linkable original service in another EU country with a clearer user benefit.
Also revisit it if an original operator's terms block the intended use, a
candidate route has closed, or a country would require more ongoing work than
its likely value justifies. Record the concrete evidence before changing the
priority or the five-country scope.
