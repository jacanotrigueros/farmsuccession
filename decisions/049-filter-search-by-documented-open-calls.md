# 049 — Filter search by documented open calls

**2026-11-26.** Extends [044](044-search-shows-periods-and-nearest-layer-first.md)
and uses the dated answers in [029](029-each-period-answers-for-itself.md).

## The question

How can a reader find what has a documented application window today
on a site whose build may be weeks old?

## What the evidence said

An Aragón grant was still returned under active resources even though its
documented 2026 call had closed. The result card already answered the call
question using the source and its consultation date. The index already carries
those periods. A source can also say that a procedure is open when read without
publishing a deadline, which cannot establish a current dated window.

## The decision

Offer a separate open-calls checkbox. The URL carries the choice and
the index only marks records with a documented closing date. In the browser
the search engine uses the same period answers as the cards and record
pages, for the reader's calendar day. A window counts on its closing day.
An explicitly closed call never counts, and a resource without a documented
window is left out. An undated call last seen open retains its dated account
on the card without qualifying for the filter. The browser refreshes across
midnight and on return to the page. Static HTML never asserts an open dated
window before the browser has checked its dates. Result cards keep the date
their official source was read. An empty filtered search offers removal
of the open-calls choice on its own.

## What it costs

The filter reads the indexed fragments of the dated candidates to check their
periods. Coverage is limited to the recorded calls with enough dated evidence.
The device provides the day. A last-minute change on an official page can
only appear after a new reading.

## What would overturn it

Evidence that the same indexed period gives different answers in the filter,
card and detail page, or that the data can support a better documented way to
find currently available undated procedures.
