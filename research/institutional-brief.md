# Farm Succession Atlas

## An evidence-linked directory for farm entry and succession in Europe

**Institutional brief - 26 October 2026**

Farm Succession Atlas is an independent, public directory of concrete routes for
people taking over, transferring or starting a farm. It brings together land and
holding banks, succession and matching services, establishment support, training,
advice, official portals and policy frameworks without replacing the authorities
and operators that provide them.

The public site is [farmsuccession.eu](https://farmsuccession.eu). The data,
method and change history are available in the
[public repository](https://github.com/jacanotrigueros/farmsuccession) under CC BY
4.0. Each record sends the reader to the original service.

### Why it exists

Useful routes are fragmented across European, national, regional and local
administrations, land agencies, chambers, cooperatives and other operators.
Existence and present availability are different questions: a legal framework,
an announced service and an open application window must not be presented as the
same thing.

The Atlas provides one territorial entry point while preserving those
distinctions. It is documentation, not eligibility advice and not a substitute
for the official source.

The current scope is the European layer plus Spain, France, Germany, Italy and
Portugal. Coverage is not a quota: a documented absence remains a result, and a
country or region is not padded to make its total resemble another.

### What makes the account auditable

Every published factual claim carries a public HTTPS source, the date it was
read and the literal words that support it. Stable identifiers keep records
comparable across editions. Research briefs preserve what each territorial pass
searched, rejected and left unresolved, so a later pass can distinguish "not
found" from "not searched".

Deterministic validation checks the schemas, territorial inheritance, evidence
links, figures, dates and trace from each published record to an admitted research
lead. Separate live checks revisit cited pages and distinguish a confirmed match
from a changed page or a page that could not be read automatically.

The 2026 edition is a named, immutable Git object. Its 101 JSON files have a
published SHA-256 manifest and a one-page retrieval and verification guide in
[`CITATION.md`](../CITATION.md).

## Practical institutional uses

- **Direct people to existing routes.** Link to a territorial or record page as
  a neutral starting point, while the original operator remains the destination.
- **Reuse a reproducible dataset.** Cite or analyse one frozen edition rather
  than an undocumented copy of the live site.
- **Audit public discoverability.** See which instruments were found, which were
  rejected, and which still lack a readable current access route.
- **Report corrections efficiently.** Supply the official page and the exact
  sentence that changes a claim; the public correction route requires no account.
- **Compare editions without inventing causes.** Stable record identifiers allow
  additions, withdrawals and changed descriptions to be identified explicitly.

### Where institutional collaboration is useful

The most valuable contribution is not endorsement or a general directory link.
It is a canonical, current page that states what a person can use, where it
applies, how to access it and, when relevant, the application period. Institutions
can help by:

1. pointing to the authoritative page for a concrete service or programme;
2. confirming whether an older or announced route is operating;
3. identifying a moved source while preserving the claim it supports;
4. reporting a missing territorial route with its public evidence; or
5. reusing and citing the frozen edition with attribution.

No institution is asked to approve the whole corpus, lend its name to the
project, share personal data or maintain a duplicate feed. A contribution changes
nothing automatically: it is read, recorded and checked under the same published
rules as every other source.

### Boundaries by design

- The Atlas does not decide eligibility, submit applications or advise on an
  individual case.
- It does not infer that a programme is open today from an old call or a legal
  basis.
- An automated source reading is not presented as human review.
- The static public site uses no cookies, analytics or browser storage and makes
  no third-party request.
- Publication is a deliberate maintainer action, separate from merging research
  or code.
- Expansion to another country requires its complete data, research brief,
  territorial model and reader-facing language support together.
